# Social Voice Guide

## Purpose

This guide is the standing writing constitution for every planned social post, playlist description, and social strategy note in `docs/socials/`.

The goal is simple:

- sound like a sharp human editor, not a content machine
- earn engagement without sounding needy
- help search and discovery without slipping into keyword sludge

## Non-Negotiables

1. No em dashes or en dashes.
2. No padded AI phrasing.
3. No generic "content voice" hype.
4. No keyword stuffing.
5. No repeating the same sentence shape over and over.
6. No hollow engagement bait with no payoff.

## AI Voice Red Flags

Cut or rewrite copy that uses patterns like these:

- "still feels"
- "built for"
- "instantly"
- "perfect"
- "unbeatable"
- "obvious click"
- "worth revisiting"
- "in the best way"
- "that tells you everything you need to know"
- "one place"
- "archive" repeated when "page", "chart", "decade", or "site" is clearer

Also avoid:

- stacked adjectives with no new information
- abstract mood words without a concrete song, year, artist, or chart fact
- fake-conversational filler like "here is the real question"
- polished symmetry that reads written rather than spoken

## Engagement Rules

1. Start with the most concrete hook.
2. Use one clear tension:
   - best song
   - wrong `#1`
   - forgotten hit
   - strongest year
   - artist who owns the year
3. Ask for one action only.
4. Prefer specifics over vibes:
   - year
   - artist
   - `#1`
   - decade
   - chart fact
5. Leave a little room for disagreement. Posts perform better when a reasonable person could answer differently.

## SEO Rules

1. Use real search phrases only where they sound natural.
2. Keep platform-specific discoverability terms near the front:
   - `Australia`
   - year
   - decade
   - `chart`
   - `#1`
   - artist name
3. Mention `Flashback Charts` only when it helps brand recall.
4. Never repeat near-identical keyword phrases in the same post.
5. For Spotify, optimise titles first, descriptions second.

## Facebook Rules

- Write like a person talking to people who already know the music.
- Aim for one compact paragraph.
- Lead with the year, artist, or chart fact.
- End with one clean prompt.
- Use nostalgia carefully. Do not overplay "remember when" every day.

## X.com Rules

- Get to the argument fast.
- Use fewer setup words.
- Make the question answerable in a reply.
- Prefer punch over polish.
- If a line sounds too tidy, roughen it slightly.

## Spotify Rules

- Keep descriptions plain and searchable.
- Do not make every description sound like it came from the same template.
- Mention `flashbackcharts.com` once.
- Let the playlist title carry most of the SEO load.
- Write for people scanning, not reading closely.

## Style Rules

- Use short sentences where possible.
- Prefer active voice.
- Use contractions when they sound natural.
- Keep punctuation simple.
- Use Aussie context when it adds relevance, but do not force slang.
- If a line could come from any nostalgia account on the internet, rewrite it.

## QA Checklist

Before approving a post or playlist description, check:

- Is there one clear hook?
- Is the phrasing concrete?
- Does it sound human out loud?
- Would someone reply with an opinion, memory, or correction?
- Are the search terms present without looking forced?
- Did we avoid AI voice giveaways?
